Recover, do not punish

A reset for shame and overwhelm

Name the state.
“I am overloaded,” “I lost track of time,” or “this task is activating shame.”
Separate fact from verdict.
“I missed a deadline” is information. “I am hopeless” is a painful conclusion, not a fact.
Repair one thing.
Send the message, reschedule, place the item in view, or write the next physical action.
Adjust the system.
Ask what cue, container, buffer, support person, or reduced step could help next time.
Foundations

Small supports that compound

Protect the body

Sleep, regular food, movement, medication as prescribed, and recovery time affect attention and emotional regulation. Build routines around realistic anchors, not ideal days.

Use people wisely

Try body doubling, a brief check-in, shared planning, or asking someone to help define the first step. Support is a strategy, not evidence of failure.

Review without blame

Once a week, empty capture points, check the calendar, choose priorities, and remove one source of friction. A system should change when your life changes.

Notice progress

Today’s momentum

A small action still counts. Check what you have done and let your brain see the evidence.
